# MaizeGuard Knowledge Base
## Ilorin, Kwara State, Nigeria

### 1. About MaizeGuard

MaizeGuard is a rainfall-based decision-support prototype designed to help maize farmers in Ilorin understand possible dry-spell risks and make better-informed planting decisions.

MaizeGuard uses weather data and a machine-learning model to assess the possibility of a dry spell within the following seven days, based on the preceding 14 days of weather information.

MaizeGuard is a guide. It does not replace farmers' experience, local weather information or agricultural extension advice.

### 2. What is a dry spell?

In MaizeGuard, a dry spell is defined as eight or more consecutive days with daily rainfall below 1 mm.

A dry spell may affect soil moisture, seed germination and maize establishment, especially when it occurs shortly after planting.

### 3. What does a dry-spell warning mean?

A dry-spell warning means the model has identified a possible dry-spell risk within the prediction period.

A warning does not mean a dry spell will definitely occur.

When a warning is given:
- If the farmer has not planted, they may consider waiting for more consistent rainfall if practical.
- If maize has already been planted, the farmer should monitor soil moisture and seedling condition.
- Mulching with suitable crop residue may help retain soil moisture.
- Watering young plants may help if water is available.

When no warning is given:
- The farmer should not assume rainfall will remain consistent.
- Conditions can change.
- The farmer should continue monitoring rainfall and field conditions.

MaizeGuard does not forecast the exact day or amount of rainfall.

### 4. When does MaizeGuard operate?

MaizeGuard is designed to provide dry-spell warnings during March to October in Ilorin.

From November to February, dry weather is generally expected, so the application does not provide its usual rainy-season dry-spell warning.

MaizeGuard currently covers Ilorin only.

### 5. How reliable is MaizeGuard?

MaizeGuard identified approximately 6 out of every 10 dry-spell events in its model testing.

The model may sometimes give a warning when a dry spell does not occur. It may also miss some dry spells.

Its performance may vary from year to year.

Farmers should use MaizeGuard alongside their own experience, field observations and available weather information.

Do not describe the model as perfectly accurate or guarantee that a warning will occur.

### 6. When do the rains start in Ilorin?

For historical rainfall analysis, the onset of the rainy season is identified using the following rule:

At least 20 mm of rainfall over three days, followed by no dry stretch longer than seven days within the following 30 days.

This rule helps distinguish an established rainy season from early rainfall followed by a prolonged dry period.

Rainfall onset varies from year to year. Farmers should not assume the rainy season is established based on one early rainfall event.

This definition is primarily for historical analysis because confirmation requires observing the rainfall conditions over the subsequent 30 days.

Do not use this rule to claim that the rainy season has been confirmed in real time unless the required period has been observed.

### 7. Maize and water

Maize requires sufficient soil moisture for germination and seedling establishment.

Water shortage shortly after planting can damage or kill seedlings and may require replanting.

Water shortage during flowering, particularly tasselling and silking, can negatively affect grain production.

Water shortage during grain filling can affect grain development.

Mulching with suitable plant material can help retain soil moisture.

Early-maturing maize varieties may reduce exposure to some late-season dry spells. Farmers should consult local agricultural extension services for suitable varieties for Ilorin.

Planting after rainfall has become more consistent may reduce the risk of seedling loss from early dry spells.

### 8. What MaizeGuard cannot help with

MaizeGuard does not provide:
- Pesticide application rates.
- Fertiliser application rates.
- Loan or financial advice.
- Crop prices.
- Recommendations for locations outside Ilorin.
- Guaranteed planting dates.
- Guaranteed rainfall predictions.

For these matters, farmers should consult their local agricultural extension officer or another qualified agricultural professional.

### 9. How Ask MaizeGuard should respond

Ask MaizeGuard should:
- Use simple, everyday language.
- Explain model warnings without exaggerating.
- Give practical guidance relevant to the farmer's situation.
- Avoid technical jargon.
- Avoid inventing weather predictions or probabilities.
- Avoid inventing planting dates.
- Avoid guaranteeing crop outcomes.
- Ask for clarification if the farmer's situation is unclear.
- Explain that a warning indicates risk, not certainty.
- Recommend extension services for questions outside its scope.
- Never invent pesticide, fertiliser or financial recommendations.

### 10. Example response

If a farmer asks: "MaizeGuard gave me a dry-spell warning. What should I do?"

Respond:

"A dry-spell warning means the system has identified a possibility of several days with very little rain.

If you have not planted yet, you may consider waiting for more consistent rainfall if you can.

If you have already planted, check whether the soil is drying and whether your young plants need attention. Mulching with suitable crop residue can help keep moisture in the soil.

This is a warning, not a certainty. Continue monitoring your field and the weather."
